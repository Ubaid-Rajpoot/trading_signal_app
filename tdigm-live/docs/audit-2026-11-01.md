# TDIGM Live correctness audit — 2026-11-01

## Scope

This audit compares the live scanner with the supplied chart references and the
stated checklist:

1. use TDIGM `(RSI 50, band 50, fast 1, slow 9)`;
2. market is trending rather than ranging;
3. price sweeps one endpoint of a completed structural leg;
4. a later candle **closes** through the opposite endpoint of that same leg (a wick is not a break);
5. that same closed candle prints a directional centre-band HVB;
6. discover the setup live and in history, preserve it while unmitigated, and
   write every confirmed signal to SQLite;
7. cover the active MEXC USDT perpetual universe and all configured timeframes
   concurrently.

The separate `bot/book` engine is retained as research code, but it is not this
strategy's decision engine.

### 2026-11-03 lifecycle-v4 addendum

The follow-up chart review tightened three boundaries after this dated audit:

* one liquidity run must sweep exactly one same-side structural level at its
  selected degree; independent/equal range highs/lows invalidate it, while clean
  directional internals contained by one outer leg remain that single leg;
* production requires repeated directional major/macro swings plus a clear local
  leg, or a fresh body close beyond the complete remembered range edge; ADX/ER
  alone cannot convert a visible range into a trend;
* an order-block fill retains the structural stop, while a breaker fill uses its
  distal edge plus the configured ATR buffer before fixed 4R is armed.

Those changes established `chain_version: 4` / `lifecycle_version: 4`.

### 2026-11-03 lifecycle-v5 exit correction

Lifecycle v5 keeps chain version 4 but replaces the sweep strategy's fixed 4R
exit. Its target is now the favorable H/L of the displacement leg beginning on
the selected sweep candle and containing the same-candle HVB/body break. Fully
closed pending candles can extend that H/L. On mitigation, the previous extreme
freezes; the mitigation candle is excluded, and it cannot award a same-candle
win because OHLC cannot prove event order. Historical lifecycle-v4 outcomes are
not mixed into current archive statistics and require deep replay.

### 2026-11-04 lifecycle-v6 direct-approach correction

The latest tick/X drawing adds a strict ordering rule before the sweep. Once the
paired opposite endpoint has formed, price must move directly to sweep the other
endpoint; any intervening pivot or back-and-forth is invalid. After a valid sweep,
price may take any path before the later opposite body break, provided it does
not sweep another independent same-side level. This is `chain_version: 5` with
`approach_model: direct_from_paired_endpoint_v1`; lifecycle version 6 isolates
its archive statistics from structurally broader lifecycle-v5 outcomes.

## Root causes found

These are observed code defects, not guesses:

| Finding | Consequence | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Shipped `strategy` was `book` | The page counted traps/displacements/mitigations instead of evaluating the requested sweep + opposite break + TDIGM rule. | Live config now uses `strategy: "sweep"`. |
| `exchange_interval()` translated MEXC `1h` to `Min60`, then `history()` tried to translate `Min60` again | MEXC futures history warm-up failed for every native timeframe. This is a direct explanation for a live feed receiving frames while producing no historical setups. | The engine/feed boundary now uses canonical intervals exactly once; regression tests cover MEXC spot and futures. |
| MEXC history made one request even when more than 2,000 bars were requested | The exchange's 2,000-row cap silently shortened the claimed history. | Contract history now pages backward, overlaps boundaries, de-duplicates, and returns up to the configured 5,000 bars. |
| Only 15m/30m/1h/4h and at most 400 symbols were configured | Small-timeframe signals and part of the futures universe were never scanned. | Auto-discover all active MEXC USDT perpetuals (`max_symbols: 2000`) and scan 1m/3m/5m/15m/30m/1h/2h/4h/1d. |
| Live websocket pumping started only after all REST warm-up completed | A full deep scan created a long blind window. | Sockets start immediately. Closed candles are buffered per warming symbol and drained in timestamp order. |
| Any non-empty HVB value passed the filter | A SHORT could pass with an upward HVB, or a LONG with a downward HVB. | HVB direction must now match setup direction; score mode also refuses to award HVB points to the opposite cross. |
| A candle that merely remained beyond an already-broken level could be called the body-break candle | False one-bar-late structure confirmations were possible. | The confirming close must transition from inside/on the level to beyond it. |
| Historical 4h/1d candles were stamped as closing after one minute | Signal/archive times and keys were early even though indicator math used the right OHLC. | Normalized candles now use the real interval duration. |
| `hvb_window: 3` contradicted the same-candle requirement | Near misses could be published as signals. | Shipped value is `0`; structure break and directional HVB must share one closed candle. |
| Untouched setups expired after 240 bars | A valid, unmitigated historical setup disappeared solely because of age. | `setup_max_bars: 0` disables age-only expiry; stop-side invalidation still applies. |
| Stored rows were shown after restart but not restored into the live state machine | A stored “valid now” row could become stale and never mitigate/invalidate. | Valid rows are rehydrated and continue advancing on new candles; a fresh historical derivation replaces the stored object by key. |
| Re-scan always reported `0` found and reset could leave signal keys deduplicated | The operator received a false scan result. | Re-scan now counts newly derived keys and clears transient dedupe state on an explicit reset. |
| Force-refresh of the exchange universe returned the cache instead of fetching | “Force” could return an empty/stale universe. | Cache condition corrected. |
| REST created a new HTTP/TLS client for every history page | Full-universe warm-up wasted sockets and time. | A bounded keep-alive HTTP pool is shared and closed at shutdown. |

## Current live defaults

- Market: MEXC USDT perpetual futures.
- Universe: every active, non-hidden, traded USDT contract returned by MEXC;
  `max_symbols: 2000` is a guard, not a top-N selection.
- Timeframes: `1m, 3m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h, 4h, 1d`.
- History: 5,000 target-timeframe candles. MEXC 3m is built from 1m; 2h is
  built from clock-aligned 1h history. Shared source windows avoid duplicate
  downloads.
- Concurrency: 24 bounded history workers; live websocket shards contain 20
  contracts each. Bounded concurrency is intentional so MEXC rate limiting does
  not destroy coverage.
- TDIGM: `50, 50, 1, 9`, centre reference = MBL, minimum close-through = 1 RSI
  point, minimum speed = 1.5× the line's recent average step.
- Trend at the original audit: external agreement plus ADX ≥ 20 / ER ≥ 0.10;
  the v4 addendum supersedes this with full remembered swing consistency plus ADX ≥ 22 / ER ≥ 0.30.
- Confluence: strict `all`; same-candle directional HVB; both LONG and SHORT.
- Persistence: SQLite WAL at a path resolved relative to `config.json`.
- Unmitigated lifetime: no age-only expiry (`setup_max_bars: 0`).

## Reading the footer

The old example

`feed: futures - 12 contracts on 1 socket(s) · 5,367 frames · 0 signals · 0 stored · 0 valid now · 0 traps → 0 displacements → 0 plans · 245 event(s) dropped by policy`

meant:

- **12 contracts / 1 socket** — only 12 contracts were subscribed in that
  running process; it was not scanning the whole MEXC futures universe.
- **5,367 frames** — websocket kline updates, including many updates to still
  forming 1m candles. Frames are not completed setups.
- **0 signals / 0 stored / 0 valid now** — no confirmed signal this run, no rows
  in SQLite, and no currently tradeable setup.
- **0 traps → 0 displacements → 0 plans** — the unrelated book engine's funnel,
  which exposed that the wrong decision engine was running.
- **245 events dropped by policy** — filtered-candidate notices, duplicates, and
  chatter hidden from the notification UI. It did not mean 245 price frames or
  confirmed signals were discarded.

The footer is now strategy-aware and says
`N sweeps → N body breaks → N TDIGM signals`; notification wording is now
“notices hidden” and explicitly states that this policy does not drop market
frames or confirmed signals.

## Follow-up: sweep proof and historical chart audit

A reported XRP 1d row exposed a presentation defect with strategy implications:
selecting an old setup always displayed the cell's latest 180 candles. A January
setup viewed in November therefore could not show its sweep or break, and the
chart did not draw a sweep marker at all. The detector itself only constructs a
`Setup` from a live `Sweep`, and requires `setup.index > sweep.index`; this was
not an HVB-only publication path. The chart made that invariant impossible to
audit.

The follow-up repair adds a second, independent boundary check before a sweep
signal can be published or stored. It requires the correctly sided swept level,
a sweep time before the body break, the opposite break level and—under the
shipped policy—the correctly directed zero-lag HVB. New records retain the sweep
candle OHLC, allowing direct proof that its high exceeded the swept high or its
low exceeded the swept low. The complete existing archive is checked at startup;
malformed rows remain in SQLite with their reasons but are quarantined as
`invalidated`. `/api/integrity` exposes the totals.

`/api/chart` now accepts the archive key and fetches up to 600 exchange candles
centred on the selected setup. It explicitly outlines the sweep and body-break /
HVB candles and shades the setup window. The canvas interaction and styling are
adapted from this repository's SMC-Radar chart (pan, horizontal/price zoom,
crosshair OHLC, time/price axes, right margin, fullscreen and setup reset).
Signal-list overflow is constrained to its own scrolling pane, so a large
archive cannot increase the chart/page height. Browser filter choices and the
selected signal are stored in local storage; server-side strategy settings
continue to be written atomically to `config.json`.

## Follow-up: exact structural-leg pairing

A BTC 2h review exposed a deeper selection defect: the swept level and the body-break
level were each selected independently. That allowed a recent high from one move to
be displayed beside a low from another move even though both prices, considered in
isolation, had been crossed correctly.

The detector now creates candidates only from completed, ordered legs. A SHORT leg
is `high time < low time`; a LONG leg is `low time < high time`. The sweep must take
the first endpoint and confirmation must cross the second endpoint of that exact
pair. Every new record persists both pivot times and is accepted only when
`swept level time < broken level time < sweep candle time < break candle time`.
Legacy rows remain readable, while versioned rows without this proof are
quarantined. The chart and evidence text identify the confirmation as a same-leg
break.

An extended liquidity-taking run no longer discards a correctly paired candidate
merely because a later candle closes beyond its first sweep extreme. The candidate
remains attached to its original leg and its structural stop advances to the
run's furthest extreme. It is removed on the wait limit, endpoint consumption,
or a second same-side structural sweep. A newer unpaired high/low cannot replace
that boundary; if the run takes it too, the candidate is invalid.

## Superseded lifecycle-v4 record: mitigation-first 4R and aggressive breakers

> This section records what lifecycle v4 implemented. Lifecycle v5 keeps its
> mitigation and breaker-stop rules but replaces the fixed exit as described in
> the addendum above.

The `invalidated` label previously had two unrelated causes: integrity quarantine,
or the stop being reached while the order-block entry was still pending. The UI
did not retain a plain-language lifecycle reason, so a correctly moving chart
could appear to have been judged as a losing trade. Every transition now stores
`state_reason`. An unmitigated stop is explicitly an unfilled invalidation with
no realised R; a zone touch followed by the stop is a mitigated `stopped` trade.

Risk/reward now starts only at mitigation. Pending rows have no target and no R.
The first proximal-edge touch of the order block, or of an eligible breaker,
freezes entry and original risk and arms exactly 4R. A later 4R touch is `win`;
a later stop is `stopped`; stop wins an ambiguous same-candle target tie. Archive
results now include only lifecycle-v4 mitigated trades and calculate
`wins / (wins + stopped)`. A lifecycle-version migration lets deep replay replace
old final 2R classifications once while preserving write-once outcomes on normal
rescans.

Book pp.88–89 define the breaker on the left of a market-structure shift and say
that an aggressive momentum shift makes it strong. In this paired-leg detector,
the candle that formed the exact endpoint being body-broken is that left-side
breaker candidate. It becomes an alternative mitigation zone only when the
directed break body is at least one preceding ATR by default and the zone lies
between displacement and the structural stop. If that breaker mitigates, v4
moves the post-fill stop to its distal edge plus the configured ATR buffer; an
order-block fill keeps the structural stop. The chart labels both zones
separately and begins the 4R trade box at mitigation rather than breakout.
